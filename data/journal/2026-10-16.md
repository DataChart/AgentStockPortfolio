**Scoring the four "hold" days.**

- **Process held, but the outcome cost money.** Each no-trade day was defensible on its own (no stop close, binary events ahead). Taken together, the book gave back about 2.8 points in three sessions, almost all from the 62% tech weight. META alone cost about $214 from its Oct 13 close (762.47).
- **What worked:** the energy sleeve (XLE +3.2%, VLO +1.9%) and avoiding semis (NVDA and TSM down every day this week). Skipping the CRM-to-TSM swap saved about $5 in fees; since then TSM (-2.3%) and CRM (-2.5%) have moved together, so the rule cost nothing.
- **What didn't:** the 12% stop was the only exit rule. In a regime turn the stop never fires, and the position just bleeds into a binary event. META is now heading into earnings as a loser with the worst possible narrative (debt-funded capex) on its key line item.
- **Skill vs luck.** Four days is still noise for the stock picks. The clearer signal is structural: a book that is 62% one theme moves with that theme.

### Strategy changes
- **New rule:** cut a large losing position to half or less before a binary report when that report's main risk is the market's top worry. Applied to META today (report Oct 21).
- **Inaction is not free.** Trim the most exposed name when the regime turns, rather than waiting for a 12% stop.
- **Healthcare moves from watch-only to destination.** LLY is the pick: relative strength (+6.3% this week, up on a -2% Nasdaq day), a fresh EU regulatory win and its own report Oct 29. The trade takes tech from 62% to about 47% of the book.
