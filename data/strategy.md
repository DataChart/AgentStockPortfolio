## Current playbook
- Concentrate: hold 3 to 6 high-conviction names. Size new buys at 15 to 35% of portfolio value. Keep cash under 10% once fully invested; with about $6 in cash, every buy has to be funded by a same-day sell.
- Favor catalysts within weeks, such as earnings beats with raised guidance, regulatory approvals, product launches and sector momentum. Prefer names showing **relative strength on down days**.
- Rotate only for a clearly better setup. A swap costs about $2 plus 0.2% of the amount. When the regime turns against a theme, trim the most exposed name instead of waiting for the 12% stop.
- Exits:
  - Cut any holding that closes 12% below cost or whose thesis breaks.
  - Let winners run while momentum holds. Once a holding is up 15% or more, raise its mental stop to breakeven.
- Event risk:
  - Don't open new positions on the day before a binary report (earnings, CPI, FOMC) unless the setup is exceptional. FOMC is Oct 27-28: no new opens on Oct 27.
  - Holding existing winners through their own earnings is fine. A losing position that is a large weight should be cut to half size or less before a binary report whose main risk is the market's top worry.

## Lessons so far
- (2026-10-21) The Oct 16 META trim and LLY buy worked so far. By the pre-report Oct 21 close, the 2 sold META shares would have been worth $22 less (697.52 vs a 708.62 fill), and LLY gained $16 (1,270.75 vs 1,258.29), so the swap is about $33 ahead of holding after its $5 cost. One data point; tonight's report decides the rest.
- (2026-10-21) Missed runs (Oct 19-20) cannot be made up with hindsight trades. A stop or rule that would have fired on a missed day is acted on at the next run's close, not back-dated.
- (2026-10-21) A single Nasdaq up day is not a regime change: Oct 19 (+1.1%) was followed by a flat Oct 20, and the 10-year kept rising.
- (2026-10-16) Four "hold" days in a tech-heavy book cost money: the book went from +2.24% (Oct 13) to -0.59%. Stops protect against disasters, not against a regime turn; when the market's main worry lands squarely on a holding's thesis, trim it.
- (2026-10-16) Relative strength on down days is the clearest signal we have: LLY (+0.4%) and XLE (+2.5%) rose on a day the Nasdaq fell 2.1%. LLY went on to a record close on Oct 20.
- (2026-10-15) A clean beat-and-raise did not save a stock in a sector-wide drawdown (TSMC). Waiting for the confirmed close avoided a loss.
- (2026-10-15) The energy hedge can fail on its own news (VLO -3.4%, XLE -1.6% on a day WTI rose 3%). It is a partial hedge, not a guaranteed one.
- (2026-10-14) A hot core CPI hit the tech-heavy book. A 62% tech weight is a large bet on rates.
- (2026-10-14) Bank earnings were sell-the-news. The "buy the reaction" rule requires a confirmed higher close.
- (2026-10-13) Avoiding semis paid off. Don't fight a sector-wide drawdown that has a live macro or competitive overhang.
