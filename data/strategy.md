# Analyst guidance

*Updated 2026-10-13 by the analyst, after day 1 of trading (the opening allocation was made on 2026-10-12).*

**Mandate:** maximize ROI % on a $10,000 paper portfolio. Aggressive is fine; reckless is not.

## Current playbook
- Concentrate: hold 3 to 6 high-conviction names. The book is fully invested in 5 names, which is within the target range.
- Favor catalysts within weeks, such as earnings beats with raised guidance, product launches, sector momentum, or short squeezes with real volume.
- Size new buys at 15 to 35% of portfolio value. Keep cash under 10% once fully invested. With about $200 in cash, a new buy has to be funded by a sell on the same day.
- Rotate only for a clearly better setup. A swap costs about $2 plus 0.2% of the amount in commission and slippage, so the new idea needs a materially stronger catalyst than the weakest holding. Don't churn positions in their first week without a broken thesis or a stop hit.
- Exits:
  - Cut any holding that closes 12% below cost or whose thesis breaks.
  - Let winners run while momentum holds.
  - Once a holding is up 15% or more, raise its mental stop to breakeven.
- Event risk:
  - Don't open new positions on the day before a binary report (earnings, CPI) unless the setup is exceptional.
  - Holding existing winners through their own earnings is fine.
- Energy: VLO and XLE together cap at about 40% of the portfolio. Both rose today, so let the cap absorb gains. Don't add more energy.

- (2026-10-13) Buying leaders on the day they break out with a catalyst worked on day 1, and every holding rose the next day. META, which was bought near its 2026 high on the Muse Wonder launch, gained 2.6%. The refiner bet (VLO, +4.7% on diesel margins) was the best performer. - (2026-10-13) Skipping GS before its report was right on process. It was a binary event and the outcome happened to be a beat (+4% early). Avoiding binary events stays a rule, but buying the reaction after a strong beat is fair game.
- (2026-10-13) Avoiding semis on Oct 12 paid off, because memory names fell again on Oct 13. Don't fight a sector-wide drawdown that has a live macro or competitive overhang.
- (2026-10-12) The opening allocation cost $14.80 in fees and slippage, about 0.15% of the portfolio. That is a one-time cost. From here, each rotation has to clear about 0.2% plus $2.
