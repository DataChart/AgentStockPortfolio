## Current playbook
- Concentrate: hold 3 to 6 high-conviction names. Size new buys at 15 to 35% of portfolio value; **no single name above 35%** at the time of a buy. One exception: a sector-fund buy limited by a sector cap may be sized to the full room under that cap if the room is at least 10% of value. Keep cash under 10% once fully invested. Cash is $3,518.23 (36.8%) after the Nov 5 close, with three holdings. That is outside the playbook, but after a -5.5% Nasdaq day there is no qualified setup; holding cash is better than forcing a buy.
- Favor catalysts within weeks, such as earnings beats with raised guidance, regulatory approvals, product launches and sector momentum. Prefer names showing **relative strength on down days**, and treat **weakness on up days** as a warning.
- Rotate only for a clearly better setup. A swap costs about $2 plus 0.2% of the amount. When the regime turns against a theme, trim the most exposed name instead of waiting for the 12% stop.
- Exits:
  - Cut any holding that closes 12% below cost or whose thesis breaks. **A close below a written sell line is executed at that close, not deferred.**
  - Let winners run while momentum holds. Once a holding is up 15% or more, raise its mental stop to breakeven.
  - Only one sell per day. When several sell rules fire on the same close, the order is: (1) any 12% stop, (2) MSFT sell-all, (3) LLY trim to half, (4) anything else. The next one is sold at the following close if its condition still holds.
- Event risk:
  - Don't open new positions on the day before a binary report (earnings, CPI, FOMC) unless the setup is exceptional. **The monthly jobs report counts as a binary report from now on.**
  - **Act on confirmed closes after a report or an event, never on overnight futures or after-hours prints.** The October jobs report is due Friday Nov 6 at 8:30 a.m. ET; only the Nov 6 close counts.
  - Holding existing winners through their own earnings is fine.

## Lessons so far
- (2026-11-05) A two-day regime test can pass right before the regime breaks. The Nasdaq rose Nov 3 and Nov 4, MSFT was re-added on Nov 4, and on Nov 5 the Nasdaq fell 5.52% on fears that the election makes the AI buildout slower and more expensive (eToro via Fox Business). The re-added MSFT shares lost about $31 in a day (the whole position about $64). Two up closes from a low aren't enough; the test now also needs a close above the level that failed.
- (2026-11-05) Cash is a position on a crash day. With 48% in cash going in, the book fell 1.93% while the S&P fell 4.36%. The cash drag noted on Nov 4 is what made Nov 5 cheap.
- (2026-11-05) A second-day confirmation filtered out a falling name. AMZN's trigger needed a second higher close that beat the S&P; it fell 2.19% instead, and no buy was made.
- (2026-11-04) A hedge sized for a war premium loses it all at once when the premium goes. Brent fell 8.7% to $94.62 on bets that Republicans losing Congress could hasten the end of the Iran war (Reuters via AsiaOne), and XLE fell 4.74% on a +1.58% S&P day. The position lost $182.86 in total, most of it after the Oct 23 add near the record. Both written exits fired on the same close, so it was sold without debate; adding to a hedge at the sector's record was the mistake.
- (2026-11-04) A first-that-qualifies buy order picks one signal and leaves the rest. On Nov 4 both the MSFT re-add and XLV qualified; only MSFT was bought, and cash rose to 48%. The order was written in advance, so the choice wasn't made on the day's move; the cost is cash drag until the next signal.
- (2026-11-04) A regime test that passes on the index can still split by industry. The Nasdaq rose 1.44% and AMZN 6.12%, but data-center operators and semis fell (Reuters via ad-hoc-news), and NVDA closed down 3.17%. Re-opening hyperscalers is not re-opening semis.
- (2026-11-03) A rotation seen on two days can split on the third. XLB rose on both Oct 30 and Nov 2, then fell 2.01% on Nov 3 as materials led the losers; XLF fell too, and only XLI kept beating the S&P. Requiring the Nov 3 leg before any Nov 4 buy filtered out two of the three funds without a loss.
- (2026-11-03) A close near a sell line is not a sell. MSFT ended Nov 2 0.53% above its 504.46 line and rose 2.74% on Nov 3 with the AI rebound. Selling early would have cost about $23 on the remaining half. Waiting for the close was the rule; that the bounce came was luck.
- (2026-11-03) A prior close used for a signal can be revised. The Nov 2 XLV close was 184.60 on MarketBeat's own table, ChartRow and ChartExchange, not the 184.65 from MarketBeat's header that we recorded. It didn't change a decision, but measure relative moves from a prior close confirmed on two sites.
- (2026-11-02) A hedge bought for a headline premium can lose it on one headline. Brent fell 7.63% to $104.77 on reports of possible US-Iran talks, and XLE fell 3.96% on a -0.51% S&P day, $114 of the book's $154 loss. A hedge has to be judged by what it is a hedge for; when that premium starts to leak, it needs a price exit, not only a macro one.
- (2026-11-02) An intraday rally is not a higher close. The Nasdaq was up as much as 1.4% and closed -1.07%. The AI test counts closes, so nothing was bought on the morning bounce.
- (2026-11-02) A legal size is not a signal. XLV's cap room (12.1%) now cleared the 10% floor, but XLV beat the S&P by only 0.39 points, short of 0.5, so no buy was made. The Oct 30 sizing fix did its job without forcing the trade.
- (2026-10-30) Rules written separately can leave a valid signal with no legal size. XLV passed its relative-strength test (+0.45% vs the S&P's -1.82%), but the 45% healthcare cap left 12.5% of the book, under the 15% minimum, so no buy was made. Check that caps, minimum sizes and the cash floor can all be met before writing a buy trigger.
- (2026-10-30) After-hours prints overstate the size of a move even when the direction holds. AMZN was about -6.5% after hours on Oct 29 and closed -1.41% on Oct 30. Acting on the after-hours print would have meant selling or skipping at the wrong price; the confirmed close still failed the test.
- (2026-10-30) The hedge paid on the risk-off day it was held for. With the S&P -1.82% and the Nasdaq -2.86%, XLE (+1.91% on oil-major beats) and LLY (+3.19%) took the book up 1.29%, the second straight up day on a falling tape.
- (2026-10-29) After-hours reactions can fully reverse, again. MSFT was about +4% after hours on Oct 28 and closed -0.16% on Oct 29. Waiting for the confirmed close kept us from re-adding about $880 at a price that faded, and the Oct 26 half-trim (532.60) ended up slightly above the post-report close.
- (2026-10-29) Holding a winner with a clear thesis through its own report paid. LLY's revenue beat and raised outlook added $195 in one day (+6.6%), lifting the book 1.37% while the Nasdaq fell 1.38%. The trim lines were written in advance, so the hold was a rule, not a hope.
- (2026-10-28) The market is paying for cash discipline, not AI growth alone. On the same evening, MSFT (beat, capex below estimate) rose about 4% after hours, while Alphabet (Cloud +103% but an EPS miss and -$9.1B free cash flow) fell about 8%. Judge AI names on how capex is funded, not only on cloud growth.
- (2026-10-28) A quiet holding can gap on no news. CRM matched the tape in the regular session and then traded about 6% lower after hours with no dated explanation. Size and stops have to allow for gaps, and the after-hours print is still not a trade signal.
- (2026-10-27) The hedge costs on risk-on days. With the Nasdaq up 1.18%, XLE (-2.05%) and CRM (-2.71%) took the book down 1.00%; over Oct 26-27 the book is -0.57% against the S&P's +1.30%. A hedge is worth holding through a binary week, but it has to be re-justified once the binary passes.
- (2026-10-27) Exits on the AI-capex scare held up: META is 4.4% below its Oct 22 sale and VLO 7.6% below its Oct 23 sale, even on a strong tech day.
- (2026-10-27) Primary quote sources can go stale after the close. Keep a written list of fallback sites and their close labels so a run can still mark on two dated sources.
- (2026-10-26) The pre-report trim is about the report, not the price. MSFT bounced 1.5% the day of its deadline trim; the rally made a better exit, not a reason to skip it. Deferring it from Oct 23 gained about $13, which was luck.
- (2026-10-23) A single refiner is a poor oil hedge. On a day when Brent rose to a six-month high and the energy sector closed at a record, VLO fell 1.6% while XLE rose 2.6%. Use the sector fund for macro hedges and single names only for company-specific catalysts.
- (2026-10-23) Pre-report profit-taking hits even the leader. LLY gave back 2.5% two days after a record close, on a day healthcare was weak. Relative strength from earlier in the week is not protection going into the report.
- (2026-10-22) The half-trim before a binary report was worth it. Selling 2 META on Oct 16 at 708.62 avoided about $120 of the post-report loss. Holding the other half "through the report" cost about another $120. When the market's top worry is the exact thing the report will reveal (capex funding), cut more than half, or all of it.
- (2026-10-22) After-hours reactions can fully reverse. META was +5.9% after hours on Oct 21 and closed -7.0% on Oct 22.
- (2026-10-22) A record quarter that is already expected is sell-the-news. VLO beat EPS by about 14% and still fell 2.4%, then another 1.6% the next day.
- (2026-10-21) Missed runs can't be made up with hindsight trades. Rules that would have fired are applied at the next run's close.
- (2026-10-21) A single Nasdaq up day is not a regime change.
- (2026-10-16) Stops protect against disasters, not against a regime turn. When the market's main worry lands squarely on a holding's thesis, trim it.
- (2026-10-16) Relative strength on down days is the clearest signal we have.
- (2026-10-15) A clean beat-and-raise did not save a stock in a sector-wide drawdown (TSMC).
- (2026-10-14) A hot core CPI hit the tech-heavy book. A 62% tech weight was a large bet on rates.
- (2026-10-14) Bank earnings were sell-the-news. "Buy the reaction" requires a confirmed higher close.
- (2026-10-13) Avoiding semis paid off. Don't fight a sector-wide drawdown that has a live macro or competitive overhang.
- (2026-10-12) The opening allocation cost $14.80 in fees and slippage, about 0.15% of the portfolio. Each rotation has to clear about 0.2% plus $2.
