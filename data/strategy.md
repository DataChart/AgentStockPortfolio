*Updated 2026-10-16 by the analyst, after day 4 of trading (the opening allocation was made on 2026-10-12).*

**Mandate:** maximize ROI % on a $10,000 paper portfolio. Aggressive is fine; reckless is not.

## Current playbook
- Concentrate: hold 3 to 6 high-conviction names. Size new buys at 15 to 35% of portfolio value. Keep cash under 10% once fully invested; with about $200 in cash, a buy has to be funded by a same-day sell.
- Favor catalysts within weeks, such as earnings beats with raised guidance, regulatory approvals, product launches and sector momentum. Prefer names showing **relative strength on down days**.
- Rotate only for a clearly better setup. A swap costs about $2 plus 0.2% of the amount. **Inaction is not free either (new):** a 62% weight in AI-exposed tech has cost about 1.5% of the book in four days while the analyst kept saying "hold". When the regime turns against a theme, trim the most exposed name instead of waiting for the 12% stop.
- Exits:
  - Cut any holding that closes 12% below cost or whose thesis breaks.
  - Let winners run while momentum holds. Once a holding is up 15% or more, raise its mental stop to breakeven.
- Event risk:
  - Don't open new positions on the day before a binary report (earnings, CPI, FOMC) unless the setup is exceptional.
  - Holding existing **winners** through their own earnings is fine. A **losing** position that is a large weight should be cut to half size or less before a binary report whose main risk is already the market's top worry (new).
- **Rates and AI-financing regime.** Hot CPI and PPI, a coin-flip October hike (FOMC Oct 27-28), and now an AI-debt scare (CoreWeave about $50B of debt, bonds yielding over 15%) hit AI spenders, not just chipmakers. On Oct 16 the Nasdaq fell 2.1% (its worst week in about 18 months) and tech fell over 3%. Don't add AI-infrastructure or AI-capex names until the Nasdaq puts in a higher close on good news.
- **"Buy the post-earnings reaction" needs a confirmed close.** Banks and TSMC both proved it.
- **Semis are off limits.** The SOX fell again; NVDA and TSM made new lows for the move. No semiconductor buys until a semi leader closes higher on good news.
- **Healthcare is now a buy destination, not watch-only.** It led the defensive rotation and LLY held up on a -2% Nasdaq day.
- Energy: VLO and XLE together cap at about 40% of the portfolio (about 36% now). Energy led on Oct 16 (XLE +2.5%) on Iran-strike talk and $100 oil, but the IEA release and Russia sanction relief cap the upside. Hold VLO into its Oct 22 report; don't add.

## Lessons so far
- (2026-10-16) Four "hold" days in a tech-heavy book cost money: the book went from +2.24% (Oct 13) to -0.59% while the analyst waited for 12% stops that were never close. Stops protect against disasters, not against a regime turn; when the market's main worry lands squarely on a holding's thesis, trim it.
- (2026-10-16) Relative strength on down days is the clearest signal we have: LLY (+0.4%) and XLE (+2.5%) rose on a day the Nasdaq fell 2.1%.
- (2026-10-15) A clean beat-and-raise did not save a stock in a sector-wide drawdown (TSMC -1.8%, then -2.3% the next day). Waiting for the confirmed close avoided a loss.
- (2026-10-15) The energy hedge can fail on its own news: VLO -3.4% and XLE -1.6% on a day WTI rose 3%. On Oct 16 it worked again (XLE +2.5% vs tech -3%). It is a partial hedge, not a guaranteed one.
- (2026-10-14) A hot core CPI hit the tech-heavy book: META -3.3%, MSFT -1.8%, CRM -0.9%. A 62% tech weight is a large bet on rates.
- (2026-10-14) Bank earnings were sell-the-news. The "buy the reaction" rule requires a confirmed higher close.
- (2026-10-13) Buying leaders on the day they break out worked on day 1, mostly luck and a rotation into energy.
- (2026-10-13) Avoiding semis paid off. Don't fight a sector-wide drawdown that has a live macro or competitive overhang.
- (2026-10-12) The opening allocation cost $14.80 in fees and slippage, about 0.15% of the portfolio. Each rotation has to clear about 0.2% plus $2.
