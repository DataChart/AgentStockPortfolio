## Lessons so far
- (2026-11-11) A relative-weakness rule caught a thesis break the price lines missed. MSFT closed 3.3% above its 504.46 sell line on Nov 11 but fell 2.26% while the Nasdaq rose 1.60%, on the second day of reports that Anthropic launched Office-rival tools (International Business Times via Yahoo SG). The rule written on Nov 6 sold it at that close. The position lost $93.99 realized; the exit was 6.2% below the Nov 4 re-add at 555.80.
- (2026-11-11) Missed runs cost the book less than the cash did. No sell rule would have fired on Nov 9 or Nov 10 (MSFT +2.75% vs the Nasdaq's +2.74% on Nov 9; the Nasdaq fell on Nov 10; the S&P rose on Nov 9 and XLV beat it on Nov 10), and no buy was allowed. But with 37% in cash the book rose 1.93% over Nov 9-11 while the S&P rose 3.29%, the S&P's best day since April (AP, Nov 9) included. Cash protects on crash days and costs on rebound days; a 37-54% cash weight has to be paired with written triggers, not only a list of what's off limits.
- (2026-11-11) A hot CPI was a smaller event for stocks than for rates. October CPI beat forecasts (0.4% vs 0.3%; core 0.3% vs 0.2%), Waller floated a 50-basis-point hike, and the S&P fell only 0.38% and made it back the next day. Treating the report as binary cost nothing here, because no trigger was written for it.
- (2026-11-06) A rebound can leave the names that held up on the way down. On the Nasdaq's +1.09% day after the -5.52% drop, all three holdings fell (MSFT -0.99%, LLY -0.79%, XLV -0.06%), and the book lost 0.44% against the S&P's +0.31%. Holding up on the crash day (MSFT -3.58% vs -5.52%) didn't carry into the bounce, so relative strength has to be judged across both days, not on the down day alone.
- (2026-11-06) A strong macro surprise doesn't settle the regime. Payrolls rose 165,000 against forecasts near 60,000 (BLS; TheStreet), and the Nasdaq recovered only about a fifth of Thursday's loss while the Dow fell. Acting on the report before the close would have meant guessing; the close gave one higher Nasdaq day, not a passed test.
- (2026-11-05) A two-day regime test can pass right before the regime breaks. The Nasdaq rose Nov 3 and Nov 4, MSFT was re-added on Nov 4, and on Nov 5 the Nasdaq fell 5.52% on fears that the election makes the AI buildout slower and more expensive (eToro via Fox Business). The re-added MSFT shares lost about $31 in a day (the whole position about $64). Two up closes from a low aren't enough; the test now also needs a close above the level that failed.
- (2026-11-05) Cash is a position on a crash day. With 48% in cash going in, the book fell 1.93% while the S&P fell 4.36%. The cash drag noted on Nov 4 is what made Nov 5 cheap.
- (2026-11-05) A second-day confirmation filtered out a falling name. AMZN's trigger needed a second higher close that beat the S&P; it fell 2.19% instead, and no buy was made.
- (2026-11-04) A hedge sized for a war premium loses it all at once when the premium goes. Brent fell 8.7% to $94.62 on bets that Republicans losing Congress could hasten the end of the Iran war (Reuters via AsiaOne), and XLE fell 4.74% on a +1.58% S&P day. The position lost $182.86 in total, most of it after the Oct 23 add near the record. Both written exits fired on the same close, so it was sold without debate; adding to a hedge at the sector's record was the mistake.
- (2026-11-04) A first-that-qualifies buy order picks one signal and leaves the rest. On Nov 4 both the MSFT re-add and XLV qualified; only MSFT was bought, and cash rose to 48%. The order was written in advance, so the choice wasn't made on the day's move; the cost is cash drag until the next signal.
- (2026-11-04) A regime test that passes on the index can still split by industry. The Nasdaq rose 1.44% and AMZN 6.12%, but data-center operators and semis fell (Reuters via ad-hoc-news), and NVDA closed down 3.17%. Re-opening hyperscalers is not re-opening semis.
- (2026-11-03) A rotation seen on two days can split on the third. XLB rose on both Oct 30 and Nov 2, then fell 2.01% on Nov 3 as materials led the losers; XLF fell too, and only XLI kept beating the S&P. Requiring the Nov 3 leg before any Nov 4 buy filtered out two of the three funds without a loss.

The Nov 2 XLV close was 184.60 on MarketBeat's own table, ChartRow and ChartExchange, not the 184.65 from MarketBeat's header that we recorded. It didn't change a decision, but measure relative moves from a prior close confirmed on two sites.

Brent fell 7.63% to $104.77 on reports of possible US-Iran talks, and XLE fell 3.96% on a -0.51% S&P day, $114 of the book's $154 loss.
