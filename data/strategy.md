## Lessons so far
- (2026-11-13) A two-day relative-strength trigger, written before a binary report and checked after it, bought on a confirmed close. AAPL beat the S&P on Nov 12 (-0.21% vs -0.35%) and Nov 13 (+0.39% vs -0.40%) and closed at a record 356.27 after a slightly softer PPI; the same test kept XLI out (it lagged both days). Whether it pays is not yet known.
- (2026-11-13) A buy can satisfy its own exit line and still sit right on top of it. AAPL was bought 0.18% above its 355.63 sell line, so an ordinary down day sells it at about a 0.4% loss to fees and price. When the next trigger is written, set the entry condition some distance above the exit line (for example, at least 1%) so a fresh buy isn't one tick from its sell.
- (2026-11-13) The binary reports moved rates more than stocks, twice. CPI (Nov 10) and PPI (Nov 13) both kept hikes priced; the S&P fell 0.38% and 0.40%. Waiting for the post-report close cost the AAPL buy about 0.4% versus buying on Nov 12, a small price for knowing the result.
- (2026-11-12) A trigger and its own exit line have to agree. AAPL's Nov 13 buy trigger needed a higher close, and the exit written with it was a close below 355.63; AAPL closed 354.88 on Nov 12, so a close just above that would have bought under the sell line. The trigger now also needs a close above 355.63. Check every new trigger against the exits written for the same position.
- (2026-11-12) One day of leadership is not relative strength, again. XLI led on Nov 11 (+1.33% vs +1.31%) and industrials led at midday on Nov 12 (Motley Fool, 12:45 PM), but XLI closed -0.89% against the S&P's -0.35%. The two-day condition dropped it before any money was spent, as it did for XLB and XLF on Nov 3.
- (2026-11-12) Keeping semis off limits paid again. NVDA fell 4.34% to 166.19 after its CEO resigned, having been down 14% at one point (AP via Yahoo Finance), and the Nasdaq fell 0.39%, resetting the AI test. The book lost 0.31% against the S&P's 0.35%.
- (2026-11-11) A relative-weakness rule caught a thesis break the price lines missed. MSFT closed 3.3% above its 504.46 sell line on Nov 11 but fell 2.26% while the Nasdaq rose 1.60%, on the second day of reports that Anthropic launched Office-rival tools (International Business Times via Yahoo SG). The rule written on Nov 6 sold it at that close. The position lost $93.99 realized; the exit was 6.2% below the Nov 4 re-add at 555.80.
- (2026-11-11) Missed runs cost the book less than the cash did. No sell rule would have fired on Nov 9 or Nov 10 (MSFT +2.75% vs the Nasdaq's +2.74% on Nov 9; the Nasdaq fell on Nov 10; the S&P rose on Nov 9 and XLV beat it on Nov 10), and no buy was allowed. But with 37% in cash the book rose 1.93% over Nov 9-11 while the S&P rose 3.29%, the S&P's best day since April (AP, Nov 9) included. Cash protects on crash days and costs on rebound days; a 37-54% cash weight has to be paired with written triggers, not only a list of what's off limits.
- (2026-11-11) A hot CPI was a smaller event for stocks than for rates. October CPI beat forecasts (0.4% vs 0.3%; core 0.3% vs 0.2%), Waller floated a 50-basis-point hike, and the S&P fell only 0.38% and made it back the next day. Treating the report as binary cost nothing here, because no trigger was written for it.
- (2026-11-06) A rebound can leave the names that held up on the way down. On the Nasdaq's +1.09% day after the -5.52% drop, all three holdings fell (MSFT -0.99%, LLY -0.79%, XLV -0.06%), and the book lost 0.44% against the S&P's +0.31%. Holding up on the crash day (MSFT -3.58% vs -5.52%) didn't carry into the bounce, so relative strength has to be judged across both days, not on the down day alone.
- (2026-11-06) A strong macro surprise doesn't settle the regime. Payrolls rose 165,000 against forecasts near 60,000 (BLS; TheStreet), and the Nasdaq recovered only about a fifth of Thursday's loss while the Dow fell.
